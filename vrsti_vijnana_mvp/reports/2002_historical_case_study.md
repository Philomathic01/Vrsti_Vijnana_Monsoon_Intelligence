# Demonstration case study: JJAS 2002 regional variation

**Data source:** supplied Homogeneous Indian Monthly Rainfall Data Sets (1871–2016), converted from source tenths of millimetres to millimetres. Each anomaly is relative to that region's 1871–1995 training-era mean. **These are observations, not forecasts.**

| Region | 2002 JJAS (mm) | 1871–95 reference (mm) | Anomaly |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| All India | 646.8 | 852.2 | -24.1% |
| Northwest India | 279.7 | 490.3 | -43.0% |
| West Central India | 729.6 | 932.2 | -21.7% |
| Northeast India | 1415.2 | 1420.5 | -0.4% |
| Central Northeast India | 721.7 | 1001.0 | -27.9% |
| Peninsular India | 431.0 | 660.7 | -34.8% |

**Explanation for judges:** Overall India had a substantial seasonal deficit; Northwest India was substantially below its own average, while Northeast India was close to its own historical average. This is a region-wise historical comparison, not district-level forecasting.

**Model lesson:** The rainfall-history-only models evaluated in the unseen-year holdout did not identify 2002's All-India deficit well (see the app's Model Lab). This makes the proposed test of additional verified climate drivers especially relevant, but **does not prove** such drivers will help.
